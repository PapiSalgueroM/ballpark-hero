## Findings and corrections

| Published claim | Correction and verification |
| --- | --- |
| 1933 to 1969 was one championship game with zero qualifying teams and no other postseason games. | The scheduled NFL title game had two entrants through 1966. Tied leaders sometimes played an extra qualifier. In 1967 the NFL adopted four division champions, two conference games and a title game. Bears history, HOF birth-of-playoffs article and actual HOF 1960s results agree. |
| Conferences later became divisions; 1933 was the first title decided on the field. | The league created Eastern and Western divisions in 1933, after the 1932 Bears/Portsmouth title decider. HOF's contemporary archival explainer and Bears history independently confirm this. |
| 1970 was the first scheduled NFL bracket. | 1967 was the four-team NFL bracket. The completed 1970 merger produced the eight-team combined-league field. HOF results distinguish the NFL and AFL sides before the merger. |
| 1978 to 1989 always had ten teams. | Split the era into 1978 to 1981, the 1982 sixteen-team exception, and the 1983 to 1989 return to ten. HOF's 1980s results explicitly describe eight teams per conference after a nine-game season; Bears history corroborates it. |
| Every division winner received a bye after the 1990 expansion. | Only the top two seeds in each conference had first round byes; the third division winner hosted seed 6. The Bears article states these seed pairings; the HOF 1990 results show the opening and divisional entrants. |
| 2002 changed only how a division winner was decided. | Four division winners plus two wild cards replaced three plus three in each conference. The total stayed twelve. Bears history and the HOF wild-card history agree. |
| The playoff field never shrank. | Removed this false harness invariant. Independent boundary fixtures now explicitly require 1982 = 16 and 1983 = 10, while keeping all continuity checks. |
| The 16-game season stayed unchanged until 2021, and 10-6 exclusions were the stated cause of the 1990 change. | Removed both unnecessary claims. Strike seasons make the first misleading. The verified archival material does not justify the old causal claim as written. |
| Overtime guarantees an offensive possession regardless of what happens. | Explain an opportunity to possess, including defensive-score exceptions. Distinguish postseason continuation from the regular-season ten-minute cap, which can expire before a reply. The 2026 rulebook is the current authority. |

The table explicitly counts the scheduled NFL championship field. It excludes extra tied-leader qualifiers and the separate AFL field before 1970. This avoids calling two title-game entrants zero, while also avoiding pretending that the number is every team that appeared in a postseason game that year. The title and introduction no longer claim to enumerate every historical rule change; the guide covers qualification shapes and major overtime milestones.

Backbone for the field changes, the 1932 precursor, tied-leader qualifiers and 1990/2020 bye differences. - [HOF: birth of NFL playoffs](https://www.profootballhof.com/news/moments-in-nfl-history-birth-of-nfl-playoffs), Jake Ray, 2026-07-08. Confirms the 1932 decider, 1933 Eastern/Western division names and title-game arrangement, with tied-leader qualifiers. The guide does not copy incidental attendance or player-stat details.
- [HOF: playoff results, 1960s](https://www.profootballhof.com/news/playoff-results-1960s). Lists the 1965 extra Western playoff, the 1966 single NFL title game and two NFL conference games for each season from 1967 through 1969. Distinguishes separate AFL competition and Super Bowls I through IV.
- [HOF: playoff results, 1980s](https://www.profootballhof.com/news/playoff-results-1980s). Its 1982 note explicitly specifies sixteen entrants, eight from each conference, seeded by record after nine regular-season games. The result rows show four opening games per conference and the return to one wild-card game per conference in 1983. No-bye and four-wins-to-the-title statements follow directly from that bracket.
- [HOF: Wild Card Golden Nuggets](https://www.profootballhof.com/news/wild-card-golden-nuggets), 2018-01-05. Independently verifies the 1970 three-plus-one and 1978 three-plus-two qualification shapes, the 1990 third wild card, and 2002 four-plus-two realignment. Its present-tense twelve-team description is used only for its historical publication era.
- [HOF: playoff results, 1990s](https://www.profootballhof.com/news/playoff-results-1990s). Confirms the third wild card began in 1990. The 1990 results show Chicago/Cincinnati playing in the first round while the other division champions enter the divisional round, consistent with the seed rule stated in Bears history.
- [HOF: The NFL goes into overtime](https://www.profootballhof.com/news/the-nfl-goes-into-overtime), 2010-03-23, and [Colts: 1958 NFL Championship](https://www.colts.com/video/the-greatest-game-ever-played-1958-nfl-championship-colts-vs-giants-20143371). Confirm the 1958 Colts/Giants game as the first NFL postseason overtime game. The old early-1940s adoption discussion was removed: HOF distinguishes late-1930s discussion from 1941 bylaws and 1946 title-game adoption. Those extra dates are outside the corrected guide's retained claims.
- [NFL: adoption of playoff OT rules in the regular season](https://www.nfl.com/news/owners-vote-to-adopt-playoff-ot-rules-in-regular-season-09000d5d827ecefd) and [Titans: 2025 Media Guide](https://static.www.nfl.com/image/upload/league/apps/league-site/media-guides/2025/TEN.pdf), Overtime History. Read Articles 3 through 5: regular-season ten-minute maximum, postseason fifteen-minute periods that continue as needed, opportunity to possess, opening defensive safety and change-of-possession handling.
