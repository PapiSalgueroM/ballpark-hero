# What Google actually receives, page by page

Generated by `scripts/auditGoogleRender.mjs` against https://douknowball.com on 2026-08-30.
Owner directive of 2026-08-30, section 6. Re-run it after any fix; the numbers
are the evidence, not the prose around them.

## The three views, and why the third one exists

- **RAW** is the HTTP response with no JavaScript: what a crawler that does not
  render sees, and what the prerendered snapshots exist to make good.
- **RENDERED** is the page after the app boots: Googlebot's second pass, and
  what the owner sees.
- **NO DATA** is the page rendered with the database refused. The directive asks
  specifically whether an API failure leaves a nearly empty page. A page that is
  excellent in a browser and hollow when a fetch fails is a failure mode nobody
  finds by looking at the site.

## Headline numbers

- Pages audited: **29**
- Raw responses under 300 words: **0** 
- Pages with no H1 in the raw HTML: **0** 
- **Pages that lose more than half their words when the database is refused: 0** 

## What this rules out, and what it leaves

Every technical explanation for "low value content" was tested and none of them
holds. Recorded here so nobody spends a round re-testing them.

**Rendering is not the problem.** All 29 audited pages return a full document
with no JavaScript: none under 300 words, none missing an H1, every one
carrying its title, canonical and structured data in the raw response. The
prerendering work from Round 256 onward is doing exactly what it was built for.

**API failure is not the problem.** This was the strongest remaining
hypothesis, because a page that is excellent in the owner's browser and hollow
when a fetch fails is invisible to everyone. Every page was rendered a second
time with the database refused outright: **zero pages lost even half their
words.** The static guide copy carries the page whether the data arrives or
not.

**Thin pages are not the problem.** Measured across all 153 shipped documents:
median 584 unique words a page, 90th percentile 970, home page 923. Every page
under 200 unique words is one of three kinds, and all three are already handled
correctly: eleven retired-game signposts that canonicalise to their
replacements, private pages that are noindexed, and four games the owner pulled
from the menus himself.

**Templated copy is not the problem.** Five-word shingle overlap across all 137
substantive pages: **no pair above 50%**, ten pairs above 30%, and only 8 of
137 pages have a closest twin above 30%. Six of those eight are the sport hubs
and the other four are noindexed retired games. Only five sentences repeat on
more than half the pages. The guide copy is genuinely written per game, not one
template with the nouns swapped.

### What the evidence does point at

The weakest indexable surface on the site is **the six sport hubs**. They are
the thinnest audited pages (521 to 614 words in raw HTML, against a site median
of 584 unique and a 90th percentile of 970) and they are the most similar pages
to each other (25% to 36% shingle overlap, the highest of any indexable
cluster). That is the profile of a page that lists links rather than saying
anything, which is exactly what the owner's directive section 15 describes when
it asks for cornerstone pages that are "strong standalone pages, not just icon
grids."

That is a finding about editorial depth, not about markup, and it lines up with
the two things the directive asks for: a real reference layer (section 4) and
cornerstone hubs (section 15). It does not prove those are what Google
objected to. Nothing available to us proves that. But it is the one weakness
the measurements actually surface, and it is worth fixing on its own merits
whatever a reviewer thinks.

### Method note

The three views come from `scripts/auditGoogleRender.mjs`, which reads the live
site and changes nothing, so it can be re-run after any fix to show movement.
The duplication figures come from a five-word shingle Jaccard comparison over
the shipped snapshots.
