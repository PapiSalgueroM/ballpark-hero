🏆 LEAGUE LEADERS · PREMIER LEAGUE

Every goal another club in your league scores goes to one of its eleven, shared out by position and rating, the same rule your own squad's goals follow. A penalty (8 goals in 100) or a direct free kick (4 in 100) goes to the side's taker, its most dangerous outfield man. About 3 goals in 100 are own goals and count for nobody, and 7 open play goals in 10 have an assist.

Worked example: a side with a striker on 84, a winger on 80, a number ten on 78, central midfielders on 76 and 75, a holder on 74 and a back four on 73 to 75. Of every 100 goals it scores, the striker 39 (12 of them from the spot or a free kick, as the taker), the winger 18, the number ten 14, the central midfielder 7, the other central midfielder 7, the holder 4, and the back four 9 between them. About 3 are own goals.

Your own players are counted off their own league lines. A man you sign in mid season starts from nought here, and what he scored for his old club stays in that club's total.

The Clean sheets board is the keepers': one for every league match his side keeps everybody out.

When the season ends, the first man on each board wins it: the golden boot, the most assists and the most clean sheets, all in your season review, with a team of the season: a 4-4-2 picked on goals plus assists (clean sheets for the keeper), so a holder or a centre back gets in on what he scored and set up. Your own players count the same way. That takes a book of the whole season with at least 73 in 100 of the other clubs' goals on record. Short of that the golden boot goes to the older scorers' race and the other two wait for next season.

Where the data on a squad is thin, the goals of the places it does not cover count for the club and no name. A save that had no book when its season began starts one at its next league match, and the boards count from that round for everybody.
