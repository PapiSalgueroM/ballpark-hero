⚔️Gauntlet Draft: NFL

Pick your offense, five cards at a time, then survive the cup.

How to play

For each of the seven starting offense slots (QB, two RB, three WR, TE) you get five real players who fit it, spread from a star to a bargain, and you keep exactly one.

Then your offense runs the gauntlet: five knockout rounds against opposition rated 79 up to 97. A level game goes to overtime, and another if it is still level.

The run is decided by the offense you drafted: the same offense always runs the same gauntlet. 16 points a round survived, the trophy lands exactly 100.

Gauntlet Draft: NFL Offense Draft Challenge

Build a seven-player offense around one quarterback, two backs, three receivers and a tight end. Take it through five rounds of playoff football in the daily or unlimited draft.

Draft a quarterback, then build his backfield and receiving group. You leave the board with one QB, two running backs, three wide receivers and a tight end.

The ratings on the cards are the ratings this game uses. A familiar name at quarterback is tempting, but the simulator takes the average of all seven picks. Check the number on the card before you start building around a favorite.

Your offense opens against a 79-rated opponent. If you keep winning, the final asks it to get past a 97. There is no play-calling between rounds, so the draft is where you make your decisions.

  • Each deal draws from different parts of the position's rating range.

  • A player who has already appeared in your draft will not be dealt again, so each receiver slot gives you new choices.

    • Five opponents and an overtime decider

    A twelve point gap at running back

    Say the second running back deal has a 90 and a 78, and you prefer the 78 because he played for your favorite team. That costs 12 points across seven cards, so it lowers the offense average by about 1.7 before rounding. Giving up the same 12 points at quarterback would have exactly the same effect.

    A tight end can tip a close matchup

    Imagine your first six picks add up to 540, an average of 90. Take a tight end rated 90 and your finished offense stays at 90. Take an 83 and it drops to 89. Either can beat the 92-rated semifinal opponent, but the stronger card gives your offense the better matchup.

    Can I move a wide receiver to tight end?

    Each of the seven deals is locked to its listed position. Save the tight end spot for the five cards that appear when you get there.

    Does a higher offense rating guarantee the win?

    A bigger rating helps, but the simulator can produce an upset. The final is rated 97, so even a lineup full of the best cards can lose.

    Will the same seven players get a different result next time?

    The same lineup produces the same playoff run. Try another set of cards in Unlimited to see how a different offense gets on.
